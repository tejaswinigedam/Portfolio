Case study

Seascanner — Product Critique & Heuristic Evaluation

A heuristic evaluation of a cruise-booking platform: 22 severity-rated issues across search, details and booking, with the business outcomes of fixing them.

Timeline
3 days · ~10 hours
Role
UX Evaluator
Public display
Academic course project: Pragmatic UX Product Critique.

The critique

Booking a cruise on Seascanner, through four travellers' eyes

A heuristic evaluation grounded in secondary research and four scenarios, with 22 issues rated by severity and tied to the outcomes fixing them should deliver.

Problem

Cruise booking is a long, multi-session decision made by very different travellers, from first-timers to retired luxury cruisers. Where does Seascanner's search, detail and booking experience let them down?

Approach

Secondary research to understand the audience, four scenarios, then a heuristic evaluation of each task with every issue severity-scored.

Outcome

22 severity-rated issues (0 critical, 5 major, 13 minor, 3 irritants, 1 cosmetic), four top recommendations, and the expected user and business outcomes of fixing them.

Scenarios

Four travellers, four ways of planning

Each scenario shaped which tasks I evaluated and which issues mattered most.

Sarah

The First-Time Planner

A 34-year-old marketing manager from New York City planning her first cruise for a winter break with her partner. She's comparing cruises to an all-inclusive resort and wants a 7-night Caribbean cruise that includes food, drinks, Wi-Fi and gratuities. She browses casually on her mobile during commutes and in the evenings.

GoalTo find and book a relaxing, all-inclusive cruise that feels easy to plan and offers clear value.

ActionShe explores different cruises on her phone over a few days, checks what's included, compares a shortlist, and books once she feels confident.

Priya

The Multi-Generational Organizer

A 45-year-old mother from Chicago planning a summer cruise for her multi-generational family, including grandparents and kids. They want to sail the Mediterranean for 7–10 nights, departing from Barcelona. She is experienced at planning trips but new to cruises.

GoalTo find a cruise with suitable cabins, activities and dining that works for her diverse family.

ActionShe browses options over multiple sessions, collects key details, discusses choices with her family, and finalizes the booking once everyone agrees.

Linda & Robert

The Veterans

A retired couple from Toronto in their late 60s, planning their next premium cruise to either Alaska or the Mediterranean. They prefer small ships, suites and immersive excursions. They plan carefully at their desktop over several weeks, comparing ships and what's included.

GoalTo select a high-comfort, well-rated luxury cruise that matches their previous cruise experiences.

ActionThey compare multiple cruise lines and itineraries and book once they are confident the experience meets their expectations.

Jonas

The Spontaneous Solo Traveler

A 26-year-old solo traveller from Berlin who wants a quick escape within the next few weeks. He browses short 3–5 night Mediterranean cruises on his phone while commuting. It's his first cruise, and he's price-sensitive and prefers to avoid paying extra as a solo traveller.

GoalTo find and book an affordable, simple solo cruise that fits his short timeline.

ActionHe checks a few cruise options on his phone, reviews key trip details, and books once he finds one that fits his dates and budget.

Every issue, rated

Major #1Itinerary is not clear day-wise4 · Major
Task
View cruise details
The issue
The itinerary is shown as a map and a stop list. There are no visual cues to assist. It lists stops by port but not grouped day-by-day, so users must read dates and times to figure out the daily plan.
Heuristic violated
Match Between System and the Real World. Travelers expect a clear daily plan: Day 1, Day 2, Day 3.
Severity 4 (3+2−1)
All users read the itinerary to plan daily activities and compare ships. The unclear day list wastes time and doesn't provide easy understanding.
Suggestion
Show a simple day-by-day view alongside the map and port list along with imagery/video media.

Major #5Booking status unclear until end4 · Major
Task
Book the cruise
The issue
Seascanner does not show the booking status (confirmed, optional, waitlist) early in the flow. Users fill out all passenger details, pick a cabin, and only learn at the end whether they can pay and secure it immediately, hold an option, or just join a waitlist.
Heuristic violated

Severity 4 (2+3−1)
Many booking users hit this. Surprise status may cause drop-offs or booking failures.
Suggestion
Show booking availability early, e.g., Confirmed vs Waitlist upfront.

Minor #1Search input feels complex for new users3 · Workaround
Task
Search for cruises
The issue
The search form shows many fields (travelers, duration, region, cruise line) upfront and many advanced filters. While not required, it can confuse new users.
Heuristic violated
Match Between System and the Real World. Assumes people think like cruise experts, uses unclear cruise-specific terms that first-time users may not easily understand.
Severity 3 (2+2−1)
Many first-time or casual travelers may struggle to understand advanced search fields. Repeat cruisers are less affected. Users can skip or trial-and-error some fields to continue.
Suggestion
Make search easier to understand by adding plain-language labels, smart defaults, or quick tips.
Minor #2Limits on number of travelers3 · Workaround
Task
Search for a cruise for a large family
The issue
In the main search, the Number of Travelers input limits users to a maximum of about 8 people. Larger families or groups cannot search for multiple cabins automatically and must send a manual message instead.
Heuristic violated
Flexibility and Efficiency of Use. Users can't easily adapt the search to real group sizes. They must do manual workarounds.
Severity 3 (1+3−1)
Only large groups face this cap. It fully blocks direct searching for them, but they can use a manual workaround.
Suggestion
Allow the search tool to accept larger groups and show possible multi-cabin combinations automatically.

Reflection

What I learned

Working on this Seascanner critique pushed me to grow well beyond identifying usability issues. One of the most important realizations was that I wasn't the user, and I had to actively work to understand people whose needs, motivations, and behaviors were very different from mine.

Because I didn't belong to the core cruise travel demographic, I couldn't rely on my own intuition. I had to dig into secondary research, segmentation data, and scenario building to ground my evaluation in reality. This helped me shift from a design-thinking lens to a product-thinking mindset, where every UX choice is tied to business value, like how unclear pricing or missing trust signals can directly impact conversions.

The biggest shift was understanding that a critique doesn't exist in isolation. I had to think beyond the scope of the immediate interface. A cruise booking platform sits inside a larger ecosystem of decision-making, research, group coordination, and long-term planning. This meant evaluating not just single flows, but entire behaviors and journeys, including what happens before and after users land on a page. It made the work more complex, but also much more meaningful.

A major growth point for me was around prioritization and severity scoring. Thanks to practice from earlier assignments, I've become much more confident and efficient at identifying what actually matters.

Where it landed

The takeaway

Across the 22 issues, the sharpest problems weren't visual defects. They were missing information at moments of commitment: no ratings on mobile, price terms with no explanation, and a booking status revealed only after the traveller had entered every detail.

The full scope of what I did
  • Researched the audience through secondary research: SimilarWeb data and four cruise-industry reports.
  • Defined four scenarios across distinct traveller segments and devices.
  • Evaluated search, cruise-detail and booking tasks against usability heuristics, recording each issue on screen.
  • Scored every issue for severity by hand, with the working shown.
  • Wrote recommendations and mapped each fix to an expected user behaviour change and business outcome.
