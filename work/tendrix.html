The Hours Behind Every Tender Submission

Problem

Focus

Understand where the manual effort comes from and explore ways to connect requirements, company documents and actions.

Outcome

A structured workflow designed to reduce repetitive work and make the process easier to execute.

Meet Arjun Verma, Tender Manager

Arjun manages twelve live tenders across three states for a mid-sized infrastructure firm. On any given day, he's the last line of defense between a hundred-page tender document and a bid his company can actually stand behind.

9:00 AMOpens his inbox to three new tender notifications and two stakeholders asking "are we bidding on this one?"
11:30 AMStarts reading a 140-page tender document, manually noting eligibility criteria and deadlines on a separate sheet.
1:15 PMCross-checks the tender's document requirements against the company's compliance folder — some certificates turn out to be outdated.
3:00 PMAssigns sections of the bid response to five teammates over email, with no single place to see who owns what.

Ideation

From problem to solution — the roads not taken

Three connected problems repeat across Arjun's week, no matter which tender he's working on — read the document, check what's missing, then turn the gaps into work. For each step, I explored a few different directions before deciding what to build.

1. How do you make sense of a 140-page tender document — its requirements, eligibility, and dates?

Manual read-through with margin notes

Arjun reads the full PDF top to bottom, highlighting clauses and eligibility criteria by hand and jotting deadlines onto a separate sheet.

  • Nothing gets summarized wrong — every word passes under his eyes.
  • Takes hours per document, and the notes live separately from the source — easy to lose track of.
AI-generated summary only

AI reads the document and returns a short paragraph summarizing what the tender is about and its key requirements.

  • Fast — gives Arjun the gist in seconds.
  • A summary can't be checked against the source — if AI misses a clause, there's no way to know without rereading the whole document anyway.
Selected
Side-by-side AI extraction — document, structured criteria, and dates

The original document sits on the left with key clauses auto-highlighted; on the right, AI pulls every requirement and eligibility criterion into a clean, structured list linked back to its source clause — no comparison yet, just what the tender actually asks for. Every date mentioned in the document is pulled out separately into both a timeline and a calendar view.

  • Keeps the source document as the reference, so nothing is taken on faith
  • Turns scattered dates buried in prose into something Arjun can actually plan around
  • Depends on accurate document parsing — an unusually formatted or poorly scanned tender can throw off highlights and dates, which Arjun still has to catch.

2. Which requirements has the company already met, and which are missing?

Manual side-by-side checklist

A split view where Arjun reads the tender text on one side and manually ticks off matching company documents on the other.

  • Full transparency — nothing hidden from Arjun's own judgment.
  • Still requires reading the entire document — doesn't reduce the core time cost.
Fully automated AI matcher

AI reads the tender and automatically marks each requirement met or unmet against the company's document library — no review step.

  • Fastest possible option — zero manual reading.
  • A wrong match on a mandatory clause could disqualify the bid with no human checkpoint.
Selected
AI-suggested matches, manually confirmed

AI pre-matches each requirement to a company document and flags gaps, but every match needs a one-tap confirm or reject from Arjun before it counts.

  • Cuts reading time dramatically
  • Keeps Arjun as the final judgment call on anything that matters
  • Still needs Arjun's attention on every item — faster attention, not zero attention.

3. How do the extracted requirements and gaps turn into a set of trackable tasks?

Build the checklist from scratch

Arjun ignores the extracted criteria and gap list, and manually types every task, owner, and due date into a spreadsheet for each new tender.

  • Nothing about the tender surprises him — he wrote every line himself.
  • Repetitive and error-prone, and throws away the extraction and gap-check work already done in steps 1 and 2.
Fixed category templates

Pick a tender category (e.g. "railways") and get a pre-built generic checklist, unrelated to what was actually extracted or flagged as missing.

  • Faster than starting from scratch.
  • Generic templates miss clauses unique to this tender — Arjun still edits it by hand, and it ignores the gaps already found.
Selected
Auto-generate tasks from the extracted checklist and gaps

Every requirement pulled out in step 1 and every gap flagged in step 2 becomes a task automatically — owner, due date, and linked document slot pre-filled — which Arjun can then edit, reassign, and reorder.

  • Builds directly on work already done — nothing gets re-read or re-typed
  • Tasks map one-to-one to the tender's actual requirements, not a generic template
  • Needs a post-generation review from Arjun to feel confident the task breakdown makes sense.

None of these choices were about picking the fanciest option — they were about deciding, case by case, how much judgment stays with Arjun and how much moves to the system. The common thread across all three: AI does the reading; Arjun keeps the decision.
