UX Case Study

Indeed

Helping retired individuals navigate re-employment

Role
Research & design
Objective
To design a solution for retired people to re-enter the workforce
Tools
Figma

Background

We’ve all seen the movie The Intern and fallen in love with Robert De Niro’s character. He showed us that retired folks can not only be extremely charismatic and driven, but also how valuable their experience is.

There is an increase in life-span with improved healthcare services and quality of life, which gives us a larger senior population.

Trends show that even when a majority of these individuals want to work after retirement, they are not able to explore further.

The challenge

Device constraints
Designing for iOS only.
Target users
Retired individuals between the ages of 55 and 65.

Process

Research

Desk research

To understand the problem space, I did some desk research that gave me the following insights.

Interviews

Conducted qualitative interviews with 5 retired individuals.

Task and observational analysis

I asked them to use the current Indeed app. The task was “Try to look for jobs and apply if you like any.” I asked them to describe what they saw and what they tried to do. This was done to understand what they comprehend and to note the pain points. (I briefed them a little on what the app is about. I gave them a signed-up app, as signing up was not my focus.)

Insights from observations

  • Scrolling through the job feed first.
  • They missed all the smaller text, which made them miss the search and filter.
  • Couldn’t navigate to find work of their interest.
  • Had trouble applying, as they got redirected to a different website to apply, and they quit at this point.

Define

Personas

Created 2 personas taking the research findings into consideration. One focus was more on learning, while the other’s was employability.

Journey map

I struggled a bit creating the journey map, as there is currently no proper journey they were following. This made me empathise with them even more.

Ideate

How might we’s and solution key points

Came up with some HMWs based on the pain points gathered from the research, to be more specific with the problem to solve. I finalized these key HMWs.

Brainstormed some solutions on the basis of the HMWs. I had other solutions in mind, such as a voice assistant and a chatbot, but decided to go ahead with these first, as those two would act better as add-ons.

  • Job preferences wizard would decrease the cognitive load of searching and filtering through one screen.
  • Video resume: inspiration was taken from prompts on Hinge. It leads to a more impactful and open persona creation on the app.
  • Job card and company insights: the seniors had low confidence in the application process, as they felt they would not be suitable. Hence the job card design changes ensure display of accommodations and their needs at work. A section showing insights about companies’ behaviour towards retired employees would help to build trust.
  • Courses: courses are found a lot these days on different platforms, but being able to see courses on a big platform like Indeed, with a claim of building skills to pursue a specific career, would be impressive.

User flow diagram

Created a basic flow with the new features and flows to understand navigation and get an idea about the different directions the flows may go in.

Design

I followed accessibility guidelines for font sizes and contrast. Each element should catch the eye and be completely readable. The stroke sizes and selection states were made distinct from default states, to highlight them easily.

The wizard was constructed from preferences that the research participants mentioned and the current app filters.

The video resume was something very experimental, but I decided to go with it. I broke it into 3 basic questions that job-seekers are always asked. Through this, not only is there an opportunity to make the UI less text-based, but I hypothesized that it would create more impact for recruiters as well. The search on the home page had to be made more accessible. The badge for retiree-friendly was fairly simple to conclude and design. For the retiree-employment card in job details, I gave weightage to stats and added a rating section to make it more trust-building.

Validate

Conducted a quick test study with 3 users to see what they think and do. I was quite happy with the results.

Reflection

Pitfalls

  • The process might be too long for some people.
  • A video resume might not be accessible for people with disabilities.

My learnings

  • Through this case study I understood the impact of UI and UX for different user types.
  • I learned to consider senior personas for future work, as they are a huge chunk of users. If we design for them, it automatically solves a lot of UX issues, as you need to pay more attention to the understanding and simplicity of each element.

What I would do differently

  • Due to lack of time, I wish I could have done more interviews to gather more insights.
  • I would also talk to the employer side to see their views on the problem space.
  • I need to practice journey maps more.
